## Summary
- Covers event, hotel, silicone, eco and UHF wristband formats for wearable access, guest services and tracking workflows.
- Helps buyers separate short-range HF or NFC wristbands from UHF tracking wristbands before deciding on material and fit.
- Works best when wear duration, water exposure, guest experience and reader type are confirmed before sampling.

## Selection Guide
- Match the band to the scenario: Multi-day festivals → disposable Tyvek or fabric bands with anti-transfer clips. Hotel resorts and waterparks → reusable silicone bands. Healthcare → disposable soft PVC with patient ID print.
- Water, heat, and wear duration: Silicone bands handle pools, spas, and repeated daily use. Eco/fabric bands are single-event disposable. UHF bands survive outdoor exposure but are selected for read range, not comfort.
- Anti-transfer and security: One-time snap closures prevent band swapping between guests. Tamper-evident clips fracture on removal. Reusable silicone bands use buckle or RFID-lock closures for multi-day stays.
- What to include in your inquiry: Event type or hotel workflow, expected wear duration, water exposure level, closure style, chip family, artwork, wrist size range (adult/child), and quantity with event date.

## FAQ
- Q: How should buyers choose between HF wristbands and UHF wristbands?
  A: HF or NFC wristbands are usually selected for access, guest services and close-range interactions, while UHF wristbands are selected when longer read range or faster multi-item capture is required.
- Q: What matters most before ordering RFID wristbands?
  A: The key decisions are event or hotel workflow, expected wear duration, material comfort, water exposure, reader compatibility, artwork method and anti-transfer or tamper needs.
- Q: Which wristband type is the safest starting point for sampling?
  A: Silicone wristbands are usually the easiest baseline sample for durability and general fit. From there, buyers can compare eco, fabric or UHF variants against the exact guest or tracking workflow.
