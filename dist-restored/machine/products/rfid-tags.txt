## Selection Guide
- Two very different tag families live here: HF laundry tags (13.56 MHz) survive 200+ wash cycles sewn into textiles. UHF tracking tags (860–960 MHz) read at 3–10 meters through vehicle windshields or on warehouse assets. Pick your path first.
- For laundry: confirm wash cycle and attachment: PPS tags handle industrial autoclaving (rated to 200 °C). Silicone tags flex with garments (wash 85 °C, press/ironing 180 °C). Textile tags sew flat into uniforms. The wash environment decides the housing.
- For vehicle and asset tracking: confirm read range: Windshield tags stick behind glass for toll or parking. Headlight stickers survive outdoor UV. LED-enabled tags let operators locate items visually from 30+ meters.
- What to include in your inquiry: Tagged item (garment, vehicle, asset), mounting method, operating temperature, required read distance, chip preference, and pilot quantity.

## FAQ
- Q: How do buyers choose between laundry tags and UHF tracking tags?
  A: Laundry tags are selected when wash durability, sterilization and textile attachment matter. UHF tracking tags are selected when longer read range, portals or fast inventory counts are more important than wash-cycle endurance.
- Q: What project details matter most before sampling RFID tags?
  A: The key inputs are item material, where the tag will be mounted, target read distance, reader type, operating environment, and whether tamper resistance or anti-transfer behavior is required.
- Q: Can one tag collection cover vehicle, laundry and industrial projects together?
  A: Not with one tag model. The collection is broad, but the right shortlist normally splits by environment: textile laundry tags, vehicle windshield or headlight labels, and UHF industrial or LED-enabled asset tags.
