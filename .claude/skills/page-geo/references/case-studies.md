# case-studies/ playbook (8 thin pages — rebuild with owner input)

B2B buyers read case studies to answer one question: "has this supplier
solved my exact problem at my scale." A case study with concrete numbers is
the highest-trust content a factory site can publish — and the easiest to
fake, which is why the integrity rules here are stricter than anywhere else.

## Target shape (STAR, buyer-oriented)

1. **Summary**: industry + region + scale + headline result in two
   sentences ("A UAE industrial laundry group tracking 200,000 tags across
   5 years with zero-defect deliveries…").
2. **Situation/problem**: the operational pain in the customer's terms.
3. **Approach**: product + chip + form factor + encoding decisions, with
   {chip:} placeholders; link the product pages used.
4. **Result table or statBar**: volumes, defect rates, reorder cadence,
   wash-cycle survival — only numbers the owner can stand behind.
5. **What transfers**: which verticals this pattern fits; link the matching
   solutions/ page.
6. **FAQ**: "can you replicate this for hospitals", "what was the MOQ",
   "how were tags encoded".

## Integrity rules (non-negotiable)

- Source material is what already exists: testimonials on the site
  (Brazil distributor, UAE laundry, Australia access-control, Serbia,
  Mexico events), shipped-order facts the owner states, and existing page
  claims. Grep for them; never invent clients, quotes, or numbers.
- Every number not already on the site gets `[OWNER-CONFIRM: <claim>]`
  inline and a line in the handoff notes. The page ships only after the
  owner clears them.
- Anonymize by default: industry + region + scale ("a 400-room resort in
  the UAE"), no company names unless the owner supplies written approval.
- No superlatives without a metric. "Zero defects across 200K tags" is a
  claim; "exceptional quality" is filler — cut it.
