# lp/ playbook (16 landing pages — rebuild mode)

These target head commercial keywords ("uhf rfid tag manufacturer", "rfid
solution provider", "rfid factory direct china"). Reality check: thin LPs do
not rank for head terms against entrenched competitors, and never will. The
honest jobs of an lp/ page are (a) paid-traffic landing with message match,
(b) long-tail variants of the head term, (c) a strong page if the domain
ever earns head-term authority. Build for those jobs.

## Target shape

1. **Proof-first, prose-light.** The visitor arrived from an ad or a
   commercial query; they are qualifying a supplier, not learning RFID.
   Order: answer-first summary → statBar (founding year, capacity, clients,
   certifications — canonical company facts) → capability/spec-menu table
   (product families × chips × MOQ × lead time) → proof section
   (certifications, QC, sample policy — link the proof pages) → FAQ.
2. **One decision CTA** repeated, matching primaryAction (sample request).
   No competing CTAs.
3. **FAQ**: objection-handling ("how do I verify your factory", "what if
   samples fail my reader", "payment terms for first orders").
4. **keywords[]**: the head term + its buyable long-tails ("uhf rfid tag
   manufacturer 902-928", "bulk uhf tags MOQ 500").
5. **Internal links**: to the certifications/QC/sample proof content and
   the two strongest matching product pages.

## Rules

- Message match beats completeness: if the slug says "uhf-rfid-tag
  -manufacturer", every section speaks UHF tags — no generic RFID tour.
- Company facts canonical-or-[OWNER-CONFIRM]; the temptation to puff
  capacity/client numbers on LPs is exactly how a site loses buyer trust.
- Don't chase 8K chars here; LPs are the one group where ~5 tight sections
  with a strong table beat a long page. The completeness rubric's length
  bar bends for lp/ — the table/FAQ/sources bars do not.
