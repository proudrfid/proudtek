## Selection Guide
- Desktop encoding or field scanning?: ACR122U plugs into USB for encoding hotel cards, writing NFC tags, or testing chips at a desk. Bluetooth scanners go into the field for inventory, event check-in, or warehouse reads.
- Match the reader to your chip: 13.56 MHz readers (ACR122U, SDK kit) work with MIFARE, DESFire, and NTAG chips. UHF readers work with EPC Gen2 tags. Make sure reader frequency matches your tags.
- SDK and OS matter for integration: The SDK reader kit ships with Windows, macOS, and Linux libraries. ACR122U supports PC/SC on most platforms. Bluetooth scanners pair with Android and iOS apps.
- What to include in your inquiry: Chip or tag type you need to read/write, host OS, desktop or mobile workflow, SDK language preference, and pilot quantity.

## FAQ
- Q: Which RFID reader should buyers start with for desktop NFC work?
  A: Desktop NFC projects usually start with ACR122U-class readers because they support common 13.56 MHz workflows, card testing and encoding on standard USB-connected systems.
- Q: When should buyers choose a Bluetooth or handheld RFID reader?
  A: Choose Bluetooth or portable readers when scanning must happen away from a desk, in field conditions, or through a mobile workflow rather than fixed USB workstations.
- Q: What should buyers confirm before sourcing RFID readers?
  A: Confirm the tag protocol, operating system, software environment, USB or wireless connection preference, and whether the project needs only reading, or both reading and writing or encoding.
