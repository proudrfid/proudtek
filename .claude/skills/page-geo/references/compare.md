# compare/ playbook (28 mid-quality pages)

Buyer state of mind: shortlist of two or three options, one decision away
from a sample request. Compare pages convert better than any other
informational format — treat them as closers, not encyclopedias.

## Target shape

1. **Verdict-first summary**: name the winner *per use case* in the first
   two sentences, with the price/spec anchor that drives the split
   ("Silicone for 3+ year reuse at $1.00-3.00; Tyvek for single-day events
   at $0.30-0.80"). Never a neutral "it depends" opener.
2. **Side-by-side table**: `Attribute | A | B (| C)` — the page's core
   asset. Every row a decision-relevant attribute (cost band, durability,
   security, MOQ, personalization, comfort/aesthetics). Glyph cells (✓/✗/—)
   render with proper touch targets; use them.
3. **"Choose A if / Choose B if"**: two short bullet clusters or a
   comparePanel. Mirror real buyer situations, not spec abstractions.
4. **Edge cases section**: when neither fits, link the third option's page.
5. **FAQ**: the literal "X vs Y" long-tails buyers type ("is desfire more
   secure than mifare classic", "tyvek vs silicone wristband cost").
6. **Links**: both/all compared product pages + the deciding guide.

## Rules

- Every number in the table must exist on (or be added with source to) the
  product pages being compared — compare pages quote, they don't originate.
  Divergent numbers between compare and product pages are a buyer-visible
  credibility bug.
- Chip names via `{chip:}` placeholders; frozen-line rules apply
  (several compare pages carry placeholders in sources/labels).
- Route trap: some "X vs Y" topics exist as blog posts or got 301'd into
  compare/ (precedent: silicone-vs-fabric-vs-woven). Confirm the canonical
  page before editing; never resurrect a redirected slug.
